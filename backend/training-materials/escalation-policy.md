# Escalation Policy

Some tickets go beyond what an agent can resolve alone. Knowing when and how to escalate is as important as resolving tickets directly.

## When to Escalate

Escalate to your team lead or an admin when:

- The issue involves a potential **security vulnerability** or data breach
- The user is reporting **data loss**
- You have been blocked for more than **one business day** without a path forward
- The ticket requires a **code change or infrastructure access** you do not have
- A user is **threatening legal action** or making a formal complaint
- The ticket has been open for longer than its priority's SLA allows

Do not escalate because a ticket is difficult or because you are unsure how to respond. Ask a colleague first.

## How to Escalate

1. Leave a comment on the ticket summarizing what you have tried and what you need.
2. Change the priority to **High** or **Critical** if it warrants it.
3. Notify your team lead directly — do not rely on them seeing the ticket.
4. Remain the assigned agent unless explicitly handed off. You are still responsible for keeping the user informed.

## Handing Off a Ticket

If a ticket is being reassigned to another agent:

- Leave a full summary comment: what the user reported, what was investigated, and what remains to be done.
- Do not leave the user without a response while the handoff happens. Send a brief note letting them know someone else will be following up.
